The last two weeks have been interesting, exciting, frustrating, and just about everything in between.

## Back on the bike

A few weeks ago, I put cardio on hold after dealing with what I first thought were migraines ([more on that here]({{ '/journal/2026/recovery-needs-a-training-plan/' | relative_url }})). The headaches hung around longer than expected, and I'm still waiting on a doctor's appointment to get a clearer picture of what's going on. The important part: it's under control, nothing serious is keeping me off the bike, and I'm clear to ride.

So the good news is that I'm back on the bike! Taking time away from training is never easy, especially when you're motivated and have some big goals ahead. I was able to keep up with strength training, but there's something about getting on a bike that the gym just can't replace.

## Big news for 2027

And speaking of big goals, I have some exciting news.

**I'm officially registered for UNBOUND Gravel 100!**

This will be my first time racing UNBOUND, and it's an event I've wanted to experience for a long time. The 200-mile race is something I'd eventually like to tackle, but the 100 is the right distance for this year. Leadville is still the big goal if the January lottery cooperates, and either way, UNBOUND will be a great chance to test my endurance, dial in my nutrition, and experience one of the biggest gravel events in the country.

I also registered for Barry-Roubaix, which will be another first for me. I've heard plenty about it over the years, and I'm glad to finally have it on my calendar.

With my first races of 2027 taking shape, I've got some much-needed motivation to get back into a consistent routine. I've also decided to take Old Man Winter off the calendar. After the recent interruption, I'd rather focus on rebuilding consistency than rush into an early-season event. There's a lot of riding ahead, and I want to set myself up for success instead of playing catch-up all year.

## Behind the windshield, and back on skis

Of course, training hasn't been the only thing going on.

I spent much of last week behind the windshield, driving roughly 2,000 miles round-trip to Denver to visit family. That's a lot of time in a truck, but it was worth it.

{% include photo.html src="/assets/img/posts/i70-drive.jpg" alt="Snow-covered Rocky Mountains ahead on the highway into the mountains" %}

I also managed to sneak in my first ski day of the season at Keystone.

It felt great to be back on skis, even if it was only for a day. There's something about getting into the mountains that always reminds me how much I enjoy winter. As much as I love cycling, skiing gives me something to look forward to when the temperatures drop.

And with ski season officially here, that means one thing back home: it's time to wax the skis.

{% include photo.html src="/assets/img/posts/ski-wax.jpg" alt="Several pairs of skis laid out on a bench, ready for waxing" %}

## The road ahead

So while the last few weeks haven't gone exactly as planned, there's plenty to look forward to. I'm hoping to get some answers on the health front, my race calendar is coming together, and ski season is officially underway.

Now I just need to stay healthy, stay consistent, and start stacking up the miles.
